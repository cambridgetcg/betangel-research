## Five principles
Each principle says what it means here and why. Where a detail is unclear, follow the principle and write down the reasoning.

**Transparency.** Every decision, bet or skip, is written down with its reason before anything is sent. Each session ends with a short plain summary. *Why:* Yu should be able to see what the code did and why, without having to trust it.

**Truth.** A strategy version is fixed before its first bet and identified by its hash. It is judged once, at its pre-set sample size, by net CLV against BSP after commission. Profit is reported at the end and never decides. In the money count, anything we cannot verify counts as a loss. In the CLV measurement, a bet we cannot measure is left out and counted as a gap, and too many gaps make the test invalid rather than better. Bad results are reported the same way as good ones. *Why:* a few hundred £2 bets are mostly luck. CLV is the fairest yardstick we have, and changing the rule half-way would make the answer meaningless. Counting what we cannot verify as lost means a gap in what we know never makes the money look better than it is, and counting the gaps means it never makes the test look stronger.

**Understanding.** Before it bets, each version states what it tests and why it is worth testing. That includes any reason to think the market misprices, or a plain statement that none is known, and what we honestly expect. A version we cannot explain does not run. *Why:* an edge nobody can explain is usually noise.

**Simplicity.** One market type, one bet per race and one fixed stake. Orders are BACK, LIMIT and LAPSE, placed before the off. Nothing else. When in doubt, leave it out. *Why:* every extra moving part is one more way to lose money by accident.

**Non-forcing.** Skipping is the default. Any doubt means skip: stale data, a missed window, an interruption or an unclear result. Nothing is ever retried or resent. Daily and lifetime numbers are ceilings, never targets. Losses are never chased. A stop waits for Yu rather than pushing on. *Why:* the market always offers another race, and a forced bet is the one most likely to be wrong.

## Money (Yu's numbers, set 2026-10-21)
Every bet is a £2 BACK and there is at most one bet per race, so the most the bets can lose is the number of bets × £2.

| | Yu's number | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Stake | £2 a bet | The most one bet can lose is £2 |
| Bets a day | at most 25 (5 until the first check, see consent 3) | The worst possible day is −£50; the code also stops at a £50 day loss |
| Bets in total | at most 495 | The worst possible total is −£990, inside Yu's £1,000 trading budget. The one-off £499 key is separate |
| Open at once | at most £20 | About ten fully matched £2 bets wait for settlement at once. Settling frees room. This sets the pace, not the worst case |
| Pause to look | at −£150, and again at each further −£150 | Placing stops, and that day's session ends, until Yu has looked and says go on. In made-up illustrative scenarios (prices 1.50–4.00, 2% commission, 495 bets, open bets at −£2), a rule with no edge got here by chance about 1–2 times in 100; that is an illustration, not a rate for this pilot. A bug that loses money tends to get here sooner, but the pause is a point to look, not a bug detector: a wrong decision can still win. A rule only slightly worse than the market usually never gets here: judging that is the CLV verdict's job |
| Betfair website loss limit | £50 a day | A backstop that sits outside our code |

- How the pause counts. The running total starts at £0 with the first bet. A settled bet counts at its verified result. A bet still waiting for its fill, or whose fill or result we cannot verify, counts at −£2. Once a checked read shows that nothing more can match, an unsettled bet counts at the part that matched (£0 if none). The levels count from £0, not from the best point reached: after some wins, or after Yu says go on, more than £150 can be lost before the next pause. There is no other running-total loss threshold (the £50 day stop and the website limit still apply each day); the 495 total is what bounds the worst case. Yu accepted this on 2026-10-21: the most that can be lost is what Yu put in.
- These numbers are for Pilot 1 as a whole. A restart, a code fix or a new strategy version continues the same counts and the same running total from the first bet. Nothing starts afresh, and a new budget is Yu's decision.
- 25 and 495 also let the test finish. 495 leaves room for about one bet in five not to count (unmatched, void, no BSP) on the way to 400 counted bets. 25 a day spreads them over at least 16 days, and the test needs 15. Ask for a re-check before changing either.
- Only Yu changes a money number: every number in this table, the bet counts included, and any £ figure in the strategy version or the config.
- After a pass, one replication comes first, still at £2 a bet but allowed more than 25 bets a day (Yu, 2026-10-21). Scaling up (a bigger stake, or staking in proportion to the bankroll) comes only after that. Each is a later pilot with its own strategy version, money numbers and Yu's consents.

## How it ends
- Placing stops at the first of these:
  - the sample is complete;
  - the bet total is reached;
  - the final date (2027-06-30) arrives;
  - Yu ends the pilot in writing (stopping one session does not end it);
  - something this page forbids happens: a bet it does not allow, a session Yu did not start, or a money number changed without Yu.
- A missed session summary or a missing explanation is reported and put right. It does not end the pilot. This never covers a record the code needs to allow, bind, count or stop a bet: a bet sent without one is something this page forbids.
- Stopping before the sample is complete is always allowed. Unless bets already placed complete the sample once they settle, it leaves the answer open, never a pass or a fail. A stop for something this page forbids makes the result invalid. The bet total is never raised to finish.
- After placing stops, open bets are cancelled or settled as usual, and AGENTS.md goes back to how it read at 416702f.

1. With £20 open at once and a £20 day stop, an 11th open £2 bet stops the session; it does not skip one race. The end is then not plain, and Yu types the release. (Under Pilot 1's caps the same history only skips races.)
2. A typed settle books that bet's worst case on the UTC day it is typed. Settle every bet on the same UTC day as its race. Six bets settled on a session day stop that day's fifth bet.
3. A bet that lost and that Betfair has already settled counts twice against the £20 day stop until Yu types its SETTLE MARKET: once in Betfair's figure, once as still open in ours. With two older bets still unsettled, a day whose first four bets all lose stops at the fifth (£8 + £4 + £8 + £2 = £22). Read in the code and run in the simulated world only: only a real session shows how fast Betfair settles. Type each settle when it is offered.
4. The gateway's own day count is 5: a manual order typed on a session day leaves 4 for the session, which then ends with a stop and not plainly.
5. **The one deadline.** After a handoff or an unknown order, that order stays open in our journal even after Betfair lapses it. While it is open, every release and every start refuses. Only a settle clears it, and a settle needs Betfair to still show the market CLOSED. Once Betfair stops returning it, v1 has no way out (note §8): no session and no price recording on this Mac until a recovery, and no typed line exists for that. The CLOSED window was seen once to last at least 23.7 hours; its real length is not known. So a stop on an unfinished bet is tidied within a day. What Yu does after each kind of stop is in the note, §18.
6. The config stays armed from REGISTER on, for as long as s1 is registered (item 11).
