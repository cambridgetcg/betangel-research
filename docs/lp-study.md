## What it measures, and why

- The question behind Yu's: when a patient resting order gets filled, who filled it? An impatient counterparty, who pays the spread to trade now, or an informed one, who trades with us because the price is about to move? Only the first pays a liquidity provider.
- Two stories, and what each predicts:
  - **"Nobody bothers."** Thin markets have wide spreads because few people quote them. Whoever fills us is in a hurry, not informed: after a fill the runner's mid moves at random, mark-outs average about 0, and the spread we earned stays ours.
  - **"Someone knows."** The spread is wide because quoting there is dangerous. Our orders fill when the price is about to go through them: after a fill the mid moves against us, mark-outs are positive (adverse), and the losses concentrate in a few markets where someone knew (the mark-outs' upper quantiles would show it).
- The evidence so far points to the second, but none of it measures this. Whelan's Betfair paper (UCD working paper WP25/22, 9 September 2025; the author's revision of 26 January 2026, karlwhelan.com/Papers/Betfair.pdf, read 2026-11-01, has the same results; soccer, ADVANCED 2022–24 data) finds matched maker bets before kick-off returned +0.6% after 2% commission, "close to zero and statistically insignificant", with a per-bet SD of 198%. He warns that those returns cover matched bets only: a would-be maker's worse offers are the ones accepted. Betfair's Data Scientists hub (Golden Rules of Automation, rule 5, fetched 2026-11-01) says the same: "losing bets are more likely to be filled and winning bets go unmatched". The research round's searches (2026-11-01) found no audited record of profitable pre-race market making on racing or greyhounds. The repository already names this question as hypothesis H03 (research/hypotheses.csv, `deferred_not_tested`) and lists its failure modes in [strategies.md](strategies.md): one side fills, adverse moves, queue priority. lp-v1 is H03's first test.
- It decides nothing. Its registered pass tests exactly two families, the mean net per decision and the mean +60 s mark-out, each over the three decision times under Holm. The discovery flag also needs the realised spread (what the spread paid us, less the move against us) not significantly negative, and the two controls; those are judged unadjusted. Every other interval is descriptive. It is not a backtest contract. It changes no existing source file, nor contract p1, backtest-p0-bsp-v1 or movement-v1. It does change the engine certificate's key again (see [What it changes elsewhere](#what-it-changes-elsewhere)).

## The orders

- **Where.** GB and IE WIN markets, event type 7, ODDS, one winner, at least 5 runners ACTIVE and listed, on the PRO tier. Off is the first-published scheduled `marketTime`, as in movement-v1; every record gets movement-v1's thirteen history gates and market predicates first, in its order, then `ladder_malformed` (a ladder entry, a `trd` entry or an rc runner id published by t that could not be read).
- **When.** At off −600, −300 and −120 s. Each decision time is an independent trial: its orders are evaluated as if they were our only orders in the market, never as a re-entry or a re-quote. In a live market the three would share one book; here they do not, so the three times are three hypothetical strategies, not one.
- **Which runner.** A runner is in band when it is ACTIVE and listed, its best back lies below its best lay, both on the CLASSIC ladder, and its best back is from 1.50 to 10.0. It is eligible when its best lay is 3 to 20 ticks above its best back. The cap, set by hand before any data, keeps out quotes too far apart to be a market: a placeholder lay at 1000 above a best back of 6.0 is over 100 ticks away, and its mid would dominate the mark-outs, their SDs and the power note. One runner per market and decision time (`shortest_eligible`): the eligible runner with the lowest best back, then the lowest best lay, then the lowest selection id. It is the shortest-priced eligible runner, which is rarely the market favourite, whose spread is usually narrower. One runner keeps one decision per market and time, which limits the dependence between decisions; CR1 by race date covers what remains, because a market's three decision times and a decision's two legs share one race date. `no_runner_in_band`, `spread_below_min` and `spread_above_max` count the markets that have no candidate, so the blind pass shows how common wide spreads are.
- **The two legs.** A £2 LAY one tick above the best back b, and a £2 BACK one tick below the best lay l, each stake being the backer's stake, as the API's `size` is. A LAY rests on the back side of the book (it is what backers can take), so it improves the best back; a BACK rests on the lay side and improves the best lay. With a spread of at least 3 ticks the LAY rests below the BACK, and both prices are new, empty levels: the files show no size at them on either side at t. So each order is first in its price's queue, and anyone who joins that level later is behind it. The orders are placed after the batch at t with no delay, rest through every message published after t up to and including C = off − 10 s, and are then cancelled. Nothing is re-priced, replaced or exited.
- **Traded volume.** PRO `rc.trd` holds price-keyed `[price, volume]` deltas of each runner's cumulative traded volume at that price. lp.py folds them by the rules `betangel_research/betfair/book.py` documents and implements (`_apply_traded`, fact F54): an entry replaces that price's volume, a volume of 0 deletes the point, and an update that would empty or zero every existing point is the documented settlement artefact (SA/4802553453457), counted and ignored; an image replaces every runner's points. A test checks the fold against book.py's own `_apply_traded` on seeded random sequences.
  - *Basis.* The files count traded volume as backer's stake × 2 (F60). Betfair's SA/360002401937 says "Traded volume (tv) is calculated as backers stake x 2", and in its worked example a runner's `tv` of 43.5 is the sum of the update's `trd` amounts (2.38 + 41.12), which carries the basis to `trd`. So £2 of backer's stake matched at a price shows as a rise of 4. The same article gives `atb` and `atl` sizes as absolute: a displayed size is backer's stake, with no × 2, which the queue rules below rely on.
  - *Not every rise is a trade.* SA/360006560978 ("Why does traded volume decrease as well as increase?", updated 2026-09-24, read 2026-11-02) and SA/360002401937 both say "Some traded volume updates ('trd') are sent due to changes in the underlying foreign currency exchange rates". Such an update may be "a new traded update being sent for a nominal amount" or "a decrease in the initial reported traded amount", and the rates are "updated once every hour a few seconds after the hour (Monday-Friday)". The repository's microstructure handoff noted this on 2026-10-09. Counting every rise as a trade would let a 1p restatement at a stale price fill a leg as a trade-through, and one at two stale prices make a round trip with no move afterwards: the "nobody bothers" signature, made by the data feed. *Inference:* if offs were spread evenly through the hour, up to about 16% of the −600 s windows (8% at −300 s, 3% at −120 s) would hold an hourly update.
  - *The rule.* Each price keeps its last published value, which a deletion or an image never resets. A rise is the amount a value exceeds it. A lower value is counted and becomes the new last value, so a currency decrease cannot hide a later trade. Messages sharing a `pt` are one batch. A rise counts as traded volume only when its batch also carries an `atb` or `atl` entry at that price for that runner (either side, any size). Only counted volume fills anything.
  - *Inference, not checked on real files:* a trade's `trd` delta arrives in the same `pt` batch as the ladder change it caused. A trade consumes the resting order it matched, so the level it traded at should change in the batch that reports it, while a restatement at a price nobody is trading changes no level. The rule rests on this. If the two arrive in different batches, the rule drops that trade. No real file has been read to check it.
  - *The blind check that decides it.* `rises_not_counted_at_touch_outside_the_hour`: rises not counted at a price that was the best back or best lay just before their batch, outside the first 60 s after a weekday hour. Currency restatements should be rare there, and the touch is where trades happen; many of them beside `rises_counted` would mean the rule drops trades and the fills understate. The blind pass also counts the rises not counted at another displayed price or at no displayed price, and how many of the counted and not-counted rises fall in those after-the-hour seconds, each beside the count expected if each rise's time were uniform over its window. Restatements should crowd into those seconds; dropped trades would not.
  - *What else the rule can miss or let through.* A trade whose level is refilled to the same size within its batch, or created and matched inside one batch with no entry sent, does not count. And a restatement that happens to share a batch with an entry at its price does count. So the blind pass splits the counted rises by what the batch's entries at their price did to that level, each entry against the level's size just before its message: lowered or deleted it (`rises_counted_level_lowered`), else raised or created it (`rises_counted_level_raised`), else left it as shown (`rises_counted_level_unchanged`). A trade lowers the level it matched unless it is refilled; many counted rises that only raise their level would be restatements the rule lets through.
- **The fill rule.** Conservative, in full or not at all, judged when each batch after t is complete, up to and including C. There are three kinds, in precedence order, so a batch that meets two counts as the first:
  - *trade-through*: the batch counts a rise at any price beyond the leg's: below our LAY's price (a backer accepted shorter odds than we offered) or above our BACK's (a layer accepted longer odds than we asked). Best-price matching would have filled us first, at our price, so the price went through us. A trade-through fills whatever its size. The files show what traded beyond us, not the size of the order that traded: its remainder may have matched at other prices or rested. So in an all-or-nothing model, filling in full errs toward the adverse fills, and requiring £2 would err the other way. The repository's paper-v1 model (betfair/paper.py) never fills by trading through, which is right for a paper executor and wrong here.
  - *quote cross*: after the batch, the leg's opposite side shows size at or through its price summing to at least queue + £2. For our LAY that means back orders (the lay side, `atl`) at or below its price; for our BACK, lay orders (`atb`) at or above its price. Those orders rest where they would have matched ours on arrival. Their size is known, so it must cover our stake. The research round's verified test design had this rule. Without it, a leg the price came to fills late, at a later trade, with its mark-out starting from the quote that has already moved, or does not fill at all: the secondary, the centre of the study, would look more benign than it is.
  - *volume at our price*: the counted volume at the leg's own price has risen since t by at least 2 × (queue + £2). The queue is the size shown at t at that price on the leg's own side. It is 0 for the main legs, so a rise of 4 fills them. Anyone trading at our price after we were there met us first.
  - The leg fills at its own price at that batch's `pt`, and its mark-outs start from the runner's quote just before that batch. Nothing published at or before t can fill a leg (the batch at t is part of the view, so of the counted volume and the queue at t), cancellations ahead never help, and nothing fills in part.
  - Main legs filled by a trade-through or a quote cross also carry the displayed impact of the filling order in their mark-out: the start is the quote before that batch, and the batch that trades through or crosses our price moves the mid itself. That is the same partly mechanical effect noted for the positive control below, and the mark-outs by fill kind show its size.
  - Legs the opposite best quote reached with less than the stake are counted: `touched_before_fill` for a filled leg touched in an earlier batch than its fill (its mark-out starts after part of the move), `touched_never_filled` for an unfilled one. So the blind pass shows how much the size requirement changes.

## What it records after each fill

- **Mark-outs: the move against us.** For each filled leg, from its start, the runner's quote just before the batch that filled it, to its quote at fill + 10, 30 and 60 s, and to BSP. Two series: the runner's mid ((best back + best lay) / 2), and the opposite best quote (the best back for a filled BACK, the best lay for a filled LAY: the side we would complete the round trip against). Each is signed so that a positive value hurts the leg (a BACK is hurt when the price lengthens, a LAY when it shortens), in CLASSIC ticks (movement-v1's `tick_position`) and in implied-probability points (100 / price). A value is undefined, and counted by reason, when the start has no two-sided quote, the member could not be read that far, the market is not OPEN or is in play at the horizon, or the runner has no two-sided quote then. A horizon after the market suspends or turns in play is undefined, so the +60 s marks of fills in the last minute before the off are often missing; the blind pass counts how often.
- **Quotes too wide to be a market.** The selection's cap (20 ticks) also holds after t. A start or horizon quote whose best lay is more than 20 CLASSIC ticks above its best back (a lay side emptied to a stray 1000, say) leaves the mark-out undefined, `spread_above_max`, counted by reason like the others. Without it, one such quote would dominate the mark-outs, the null control and the power note. It is judged on the quote, so it applies to every series, and to the null control's start and horizon quotes too.
- **Edge at fill.** How far the leg's own price lies from the start mid in its favour: at a 3-tick spread the mid is 1.5 ticks from each best price and our order one tick inside, so half a tick. It is undefined with no start mid, or with a start quote too wide (`spread_above_max`). *Inference:* a one-tick move against us after a fill wipes out about twice a leg's edge.
- **Realised spread.** The edge at fill less the move of the mid against us at a horizon, in points, over the legs with both defined: what the spread paid us, after the price moved. At the secondary's horizon (+60 s) it decides the flag's condition (below).
- **The positive control: joining instead of improving.** At every eligible decision, two more £2 legs on the same runner join the best prices: a LAY at b and a BACK at l, each queued behind the size shown at t. Same fill rule, same mark-outs; they never enter the net. Orders behind a queue at the touch fill mostly when the price comes through them, so a measure that can see adverse selection should see it here. Part of that is mechanical: a join leg filled by a trade-through or a quote cross is marked from the quote before its batch, and the batch that consumes or crosses the touch moves the mid itself. So the control checks the signs and timing of the mark-out code; it does not show that the main legs could detect a small effect.
- **The null control: random times.** For every filled main leg, a time u drawn uniformly from the whole milliseconds of (t, C] (`metrics.HashStream`, seed 3, a label of market id, decision time and side), and the mid's move against that leg from u to u + 10, 30 and 60 s and to BSP. It is tied to no fill, so with no drift it averages 0. A start at u when the member is unreadable, the market not OPEN or in play is undefined for that reason, and one with a quote too wide `spread_above_max`. The registered pass also reports, descriptively, its mean move at BSP beside the main mark-out at BSP over the same legs.
  - *Inference:* a uniform u samples the whole window evenly, while fills crowd towards the off. Slow drift that varies through the window (faster near the off, say) can leave the null's mean small enough to pass the kill rule while still reaching the filled legs' mark-outs. A null that does not block is not proof that the main mark-out is free of drift.

## Valuation

- For each eligible decision, the market P&L of its filled main legs in each outcome of the selected runner, stake s = £2:
  - a filled BACK at B: +s(B − 1) if the runner wins, −s if it loses;
  - a filled LAY at L: −s(L − 1) if it wins, +s if it loses;
  - no fill: exactly 0.
- Commission is 2% (the Basic package, [backtest plan](backtest-plan.md) §8) of an outcome's net when it is positive, rounded to the nearest penny with a half penny rounded up. Exchange General Rules, "Results and market settlement" (read 2026-11-01 through a reader proxy): "£3.335 will be settled as £3.34". So a market whose net is under £0.25 pays £0.00. *Inference:* a one-tick £2 round trip wins 2 × the tick, so every one below 6.0 pays nothing and one from 6.0 to 10.0 pays 1p. Each decision is one market for commission, as if its legs were our only bets in it. The repository's P0 contract rounds commission up per market (`per_market_ceil_penny`); that is a pessimistic accounting convention, and this study uses Betfair's own rule instead.
- The decision's net is the expectation of its two outcomes' nets after commission under p = 1/BSP, BSP the selected runner's valid BSP (movement-v1's `bsp_valid_if`), as contract p1's NCLV assumes. Unrounded, each case has a closed form:
  - **A lone BACK at B** (inventory at the off): p·s(B − 1)(1 − c) − (1 − p)s = s[(1 + (B − 1)(1 − c))/BSP − 1], contract p1's NCLV times s.
  - **A lone LAY at L**, its analogue: the layer wins s, less commission cs, when the runner loses (probability 1 − p), and pays s(L − 1) when it wins. So the value is (1 − p)s(1 − c) − p·s(L − 1) = s[(1 − c) − p(1 − c + L − 1)] = s[(1 − c) − (L − c)/BSP]. At L = BSP this is −s·c(BSP − 1)/BSP, the same cost a BACK at P = BSP pays. So neither side is favoured at the fair price, but only if BSP is calibrated: 1/BSP need not sum to 1 over a market (its overround at BSP), and every p here is the raw 1/BSP.
  - **A completed round trip**, a BACK at B above a LAY at L, both £2: s(B − 1) − s(L − 1) = s(B − L) if the runner wins and −s + s = 0 if it loses, so it is worth s(B − L)(1 − c)/BSP. Equal stakes lock the spread as a payoff that never loses, paid only if the runner wins; a greened lay stake of s·B/L would pay s(B/L − 1) either way. *Inference:* a one-tick round trip at 5.0 is worth about 4p of £2 in expectation.
- **The overround, descriptive.** The registered pass also gives the mean net over every eligible decision with p normalised by the market's book at BSP: p = (1/BSP) / Σ 1/BSP over the market's runners with a valid BSP. The index record holds every runner's final BSP, so no BSP is guessed. It takes the overround out of p; it never decides anything.
- Both valuations are reported: the registered pass gives the mean net with rounded commission and, beside it, with unrounded commission, and both passes count the rounding effects (`exact`, `rounded_up`, `rounded_down`, `rounded_to_zero`, or no positive outcome). An outcome-based (settled) valuation is out of scope: no settlement rule is merged, and settlement v2 is a draft (branch `lab/settlement-v2`).

## Classes and exclusions

- Each issued decision ends in one class. First movement-v1's outcome classes, for the selected runner and the runners ACTIVE at t: `outcome_unknown`, `void_own_runner_removed`, `post_decision_removal`, `result_revised_after_close`, `missing_bsp`. Then the window (t, C]: `window_unknown`, `removal_in_window`, `suspension_in_window` (the status at t, or any status published in the window, is not OPEN), `in_play_in_window`, `schedule_revised_in_window` (the `marketTime` changed after t; at or before t it is already `schedule_revised_before_decision`), `stream_malformed_in_window`. Only `eligible` decisions enter any statistic. The first three window checks are movement-v1's `window_problem`.
- Removals are classes, as in movement-v1, so no fill made before a removal is ever valued as standing. The outcome classes come first, so `removal_in_window` only catches a runner that was not ACTIVE at t (one HIDDEN, say).

## Tests, controls and the flag

- **Primary.** Mean net per decision over every eligible decision (an unfilled one counts 0), at each decision time, two-sided against 0, CR1 by race date with t(G − 1), tested only with at least 200 decisions on 15 race dates. Holm over the three decision times at a familywise 0.05.
- **Secondary.** Mean +60 s mid move against us, in points, over the filled main legs with a defined value (both legs of a round trip count), with the same test and its own Holm family. A rejection with a positive mean is reported as `adverse_selection_detected`: the study's answer to "who fills us". It does not enter the flag.
  - *What "not detected" means.* The registered pass reports the secondary's CR1 interval upper bound at each decision time. A secondary not detected means only that the mean move against us is plausibly below that bound (95%, unadjusted); it never supports story (a) on its own.
  - *Beside it, descriptive:* the mean mark-out at BSP over the same filled main legs, with its CR1 interval, not under Holm and never deciding. It is defined whenever the start is, so it keeps the late fills that the +60 s horizon loses (see [What it cannot show](#what-it-cannot-show)).
- **Realised spread.** The edge at fill less the +60 s mid move against us, in points, over the filled main legs with both defined, with the tests' CR1 test and gates (at least 200 legs on 15 race dates). It decides only the flag's condition, judged unadjusted and never under Holm: it holds when the two-sided p is at least 0.05 or the mean is not below 0. An untestable realised spread fails it, and so does an SE of 0 with a mean below 0.
- **Below the gates, counts only.** A test with fewer than 200 values or 15 race dates is `underpowered`: it reports its counts and no mean, SE or interval. So does every descriptive group over exactly its decisions or legs, in both passes, with the same reason (`underpowered`) and no SD or quantile either: net over every eligible decision (with either commission and with p normalised by the book), the +60 s mid mark-outs over all the main legs or all the join legs, the realised spread, and any other group that happens to hold the same ones (an opposite series, say, or a fill kind that holds every leg). Any other descriptive group with fewer than 100 values reports counts only (`counts_only`).
- **Controls, and what their failure would mean.**
  - The positive control shows adverse selection at a decision time when its +60 s mean move against us is above 0 with an unadjusted p below 0.05. If it does not, either the mark-out code is wrong (a sign or timing error) or these files cannot show adverse selection at +60 s; either way a main result of "not adverse" would be no evidence that our fills are benign. Because part of its success is mechanical (above), passing it is weak evidence of the measure's power; failing it is strong evidence of a defect.
  - The null control blocks the flag when it cannot be tested, when the main mark-out over the same legs cannot be, or when its absolute mean is at least half the main's over the same legs and its own p is below 0.05 or undefined: movement-v1's kill rule. A null away from 0 means the selected runners' mids drift (for example shortening into the off) in a way that helps or hurts the legs that fill, so the main mark-out mixes drift with selection and cannot be read either way.
- **The discovery flag**, per decision time, for people to read and never a decision: the primary rejected under Holm with a positive mean, its CR1 lower bound above 0, the realised spread not significantly negative, the positive control showing adverse selection, and the null control not blocking. The study's question is whether the spread pays for adverse selection, so the flag asks that and not whether there is any. An earlier draft also required the +60 s mark-out not significantly adverse; that made the flag story (a) alone, and nearly out of reach, since a mark-out that starts from the quote before the filling batch is partly adverse by construction. How to read a flag is under [Predictions](#predictions-signed-before-any-data).
- **Power, computed blind.** For a mean of N decisions with per-decision SD sd on G race dates, the minimum detectable effect at Holm's first step and 80% power is (t(1 − 0.05/6; G − 1) + t(0.8; G − 1)) × sd/√N, under independence; clustering makes it larger. The blind pass prints it from its own released SDs and counts, never from a location: for the primary with sd = √f × SD over decisions with a fill, f the share with a fill (it leaves out a term in the square of those decisions' own mean, small unless the effect is large), and for the secondary from the SD of its own values, the +60 s mid mark-out in points over every filled main leg (both sides pooled, so the ticks-only release by side leaves it in place), which, like the secondary, needs 200 legs on 15 race dates. *Inference, assumed inputs, not data:* N = 2,000 decisions on 300 race dates, f = 0.4 and an SD of £0.25 per filled decision give sd ≈ £0.158 and a detectable effect of 3.25 × 0.158/√2000 ≈ £0.0115 per decision, 0.6% of the stake; 1,600 legs with a +60 s SD of 1 point give about 0.081 points.

## Diagnostics

Counts only, in both passes. They change no rule; one that shows a rule wrong leads to a fix, a new registration and a new blind pass.

- Per decision time and role: `touched_before_fill` and `touched_never_filled`; fills by kind within 1 and 2 s of t (fills a slower connection could change); and fills by kind in the first 60 s after a whole UTC hour, Monday to Friday, beside the count expected if each fill's time were uniform over its window. Fill times are not uniform (fills crowd towards the off), so this is a flag, not a test.
- Per decision time, over the eligible windows, the selected runner's traded-volume changes: rises counted, split by what the batch's entries at their price did to that level (lowered or deleted, raised or created, left as shown); rises not counted by where their price was, with those at the touch outside the after-the-hour seconds apart (the blind check of the traded-volume rule); how many counted and not-counted rises fell in those after-the-hour seconds, each beside the count expected if each rise's time were uniform over its window; values below their price's last published value; and rc entries raising three or more prices that are not one run of adjacent ticks. A restatement can raise scattered prices at once; a trade sweeping the book raises adjacent ones.
- Per decision time, the fills by kind of the issued decisions of each excluded class (see [What it cannot show](#what-it-cannot-show)).
- Per pass, the replayed markets with a null ladder update, a `trd` settlement-zeroing frame or a `trd` value below its price's last published value.

## Two passes

1. **Blind pass.** It releases counts by code and class; coverage by registered month; eligible decisions by decision time × spread bucket (3–4, 5–7, 8–20 ticks) × price band (1.50–2.98, 3.00–4.90, 5.0–10.0) × matched-volume tercile, with the cut points rounded to 2 significant figures; for the main and the join legs, fills by side and kind, fill rates per leg and of both (round-trip completion), inventory-state counts and the seconds to each fill; the diagnostics; the rounding counts; the power figures; and, for net per decision over the decisions with a fill, for every mark-out (main legs all, by side, by fill kind and touched before their fill; join; null), for the edge at fill (main legs only, the same groups; never the join or null legs) and for the realised spread at +60 s (main legs, both sides pooled), exactly two statistics: the SD and the symmetric nearest-rank ranges q95 − q05, q90 − q10 and q75 − q25, rounded to 4 decimal places. The groups by side (LAY, BACK) give them in ticks only, their points as counts only; every group that pools both sides gives both units. Net by inventory state (lay only, back only, round trip) is counts only.

   Why only these. A shift leaves an SD and a symmetric range as they are, and so (but for nearest-rank ties) does a change of sign, so they keep a group's spread but neither where it lies nor which way. *Inference:* when many values sit exactly at 0, the spread itself depends in part on how far the rest lie from 0, so an SD or a range can carry the size of a group's mean, never its sign. Quantiles less the median are not released: many mark-outs are exactly 0 (an unchanged mid), and with that atom at the median, quantiles less the median are the raw quantiles, which show the direction. Net is described only over decisions with at least one fill, never over every eligible decision: the unfilled decisions sit exactly at 0, so an SD over all of them together with the SD over the filled ones would give, by the law of total variance, the size of the filled decisions' mean. The same law, with the counts, gives the dispersion between the locations of a group's parts, and that says where none lies unless one part's location is known beforehand. A round trip's net is such a part (a known positive function of its prices), so with the states' SDs a guess at the round trips' mean would bound the filled decisions' mean; that is why net by inventory state is counts only here. The mark-out, edge and realised-spread groups give the size of the gaps between their parts (LAY and BACK, the fill kinds, touched or not), not where any lies.

   By side, the blind pass gives ticks only: within each tick band of the ladder a tick is worth more points the shorter the price (100/price is convex), and a shortening hurts a LAY while a lengthening hurts a BACK, so the LAY group's points SD over its ticks SD, beside the BACK group's, tracks the sign of the side's mark-out (*inference*, from a synthetic probe only: that ratio of ratios was about 1.00–1.05 when the mid moved 2–5 ticks against the legs on average and about 0.95–1.00 when it moved as far in their favour, on 800 legs a side).

   It releases no mean, median, quantile or other location of net, of a mark-out, of an edge or of the realised spread, no points statistic of a group by side, and no test, control effect or flag.
2. **Registered pass.** It runs once, after people have read the blind tables, at the same commit and backtest sources, with the blind receipt of the same registration and the same input, whatever the blind tables show. Only an engineering failure stops it. Any stop between the blind and registered passes, its reason and the commit that fixes it are written into the body of the next PR; then comes a new blind pass, and a new registration if this file changes. It adds the quantiles as they are, net by inventory state, the groups by side in points, the mean with its CR1 interval for every group the blind pass gives an SD for and for these two, the primary over every eligible decision (and, descriptive, with unrounded commission and with p normalised by the market's book), the two families' tests with Holm and the secondary's upper bound, the mark-out at BSP beside the secondary, the realised spread's test, the controls (with the null at BSP) and the flags.

Both passes refuse unless the registration file's sha256 equals the one given on the command line, which the lead pins from the merged commit. A group with fewer than 100 values reports counts only, and so do a test below its gates (200 values on 15 race dates) and every group over exactly its decisions or legs.

## Predictions, signed before any data

*Inference, judgement and not evidence (the research round's verifier marked its numeric prior as unsupported, the direction as well supported):*

- (a) **The spread is rent.** Mark-outs at +60 s and at BSP about 0, round trips common enough, net per decision above 0.
- (b) **The spread is fair pay for risk.** Mark-outs eat about the edge; realised spread about 0; net slightly negative after commission and the inventory left at the off.
- (c) **We are picked off.** Trade-through and quote-cross fills dominate, mark-outs are worse than half a tick, and lone legs at the off lose more than round trips earn.

The research round put (c) first and (a) last, on Whelan's finding that makers are about break-even and worse where spreads are wide, on our lack of any speed advantage, and on the edge being half a tick per leg. One unpaired £2 leg priced a little off BSP can cost about as much as four one-tick round trips earn (*inference*, the maker-evidence note's arithmetic).

**How to read a flag.** The flag asks whether the spread pays for adverse selection, not whether there is any (the primary positive, the realised spread not significantly negative, the controls passing). Read it with `adverse_selection_detected` beside it:

- A flag without `adverse_selection_detected` fits (a), the spread is rent. Even then the secondary's "not detected" means only that the mean move against us is plausibly below its upper bound (95%, unadjusted); it is not proof that fills are benign.
- A flag with `adverse_selection_detected` is a fourth reading, (d) **picked off but paid for it**: informed traders do fill us and the mid does move against us, but the spread we earned outlasts the move. It is neither (a) nor (c).
- Without a flag, the tables, not the flag, say whether (b) or (c) fits better.

## What it cannot show

- **Virtual prices.** The historical ladders exclude virtual (cross-matched) prices. The Exchange Stream API documents its best offers as "Best prices not including Virtual Bets", with a separate field that adds them (developer docs page 2687396, version of 2026-10-20), and Betfair's Data Scientists say "Cross matching (Virtual Bets) is also not included in the historic stream files" ("Simulating Past Betfair Markets", fetched 2026-11-01, undated; they expect limited impact for racing, unquantified). So some spreads look wider here than live, and part of the 3 to 20 tick population may not exist in the live book.
- **Queue position.** No tier shows it. The rule puts us first at a level we created and last behind everything shown at one we joined, and cannot see traders who improve in front of us after we post, cancellations, or our own effect on others. That leaves an optimistic bias that only live fills, compared with modelled fills for the same decisions, can measure.
- **Latency.** The orders rest from the batch at t with no delay, and never cancel early. A home connection would place later and could not pull a quote ahead of a fast trader. The research round's design had a 1 s delay (2 s as a check); this build keeps none and counts the fills within 1 and 2 s of t, which a delay could change. Modelling the delay (the level may no longer be empty when the order arrives) would be its own registration.
- **Exclusions that look ahead.** A decision is excluded when, after t, a runner is removed, the market suspends or turns in play, or the schedule is revised. Those are the windows in which the best-informed traders act, for example before a withdrawal or an early off. So the eligible population leaves out some of the fills most likely to be adverse, and the result is flattered by an unknown amount. The blind pass counts the excluded decisions' fills by class and kind, so the size of what is removed is visible; they are not valued, because a removal voids or reprices the bets and these files cannot say how.
- **Late fills and the +60 s horizon.** C is off − 10 s, so a fill after about off − 70 s has its +60 s horizon in the last seconds before the off or after it, when the market is often suspended or in play, and its +60 s value is then undefined. The secondary therefore leaves out the latest fills, where informed trading is likeliest (*inference*), and leans benign; most at −120 s, whose whole window is the last 110 s. The descriptive mark-out at BSP beside it keeps them.
- **Partial fills.** Every fill is all or nothing. A trade-through fills £2 whatever traded, and a quote cross or volume at our price must cover £2. Small fills are therefore overstated on the trade-through side and missed on the other two.
- **Today's market.** 2015–16 liquidity and participants differ from today's; every figure is discovery material, never proof of a current edge. These months count as inspected from P0's first run (backtest plan §3, [movement-study.md](movement-study.md)), and the study applies today's 2% Basic rate.
- **Live caps.** A £2 LAY at L risks £2(L − 1): £10.40 at 6.2, £18 at 10.0. The pilot's £2 liability cap ([engineering note](betfair-pilot-engineering.md) §9) would allow a £2 lay only up to 2.0 (a £1 lay up to 3.0); the gateway's default £10 per order stops above 6.0. RS-19 allows one £2 BACK, LIMIT, LAPSE per race ([pilot policy](betfair-pilot-policy.md)), and the engineering note §13 still forbids lays and a second decision in a market. So any live test needs a new contract, Yu's money numbers for lays, two resting orders per race and an RS-19 change, plus a cancel-before-off rule that does not rely on LAPSE, which acts only at turn-in-play, nor on the Heartbeat API, which cancels every LIMIT bet on the account (F58). Sizing the lay by liability would also change the round-trip arithmetic.
- **Transaction charges.** Not modelled: Betfair charges them only above 5,000 qualifying transactions in a clock hour (Betfair Charges §6, read 2026-11-01), far beyond a £2 study's scale.
- **BSP calibration.** The valuation assumes 1/BSP is a calibrated win probability, as contract p1 does. This study does not test it. The descriptive net with p normalised by the market's book at BSP shows how much the overround alone moves the result.
- **Void markets.** The files have no market-level void flag; only removals are seen.
- **Settled results.** No outcome-based valuation is made.
- **A single registered pass.** The code enforces the order of the passes on one machine, and the same code in both, not that the registered pass runs only once. Running it once is the operator's rule.

## Greyhounds and other registrations

- Event type, countries, market type, betting type, number of winners and the minimum field are the registration's values. A greyhound registration (eventTypeId 4339) would be a new file with a new sha256 and no code change, if its data are like these: PRO files (full `atb`/`atl` and tick-level `trd`; the counting rule assumes, as an *inference* the blind pass checks, that a trade's `trd` delta arrives in the same batch as the level change it made; F60 documents the × 2 basis for PRO historical data, not per sport), ingested and indexed under their own data root, on the CLASSIC ladder (assumed, and every price checked), with `bspMarket`, `bspReconciled` and runner `bsp`. GB races have at most six traps, so a minimum of 5 ACTIVE runners drops races with two vacant traps; a greyhound registration may want 4 or 5, which is its own decision.
- What it needs first: greyhound files (the lab holds none; Yu downloads data); the backtest plan's capability audit for a new sport (§2.7: the archive and index code has never read a greyhound file); and Yu's decision to reopen greyhounds. Betfair's Greyhound Racing Rules void every bet matched before a non-runner or reserve update; a removal after t already classes the decision `void_own_runner_removed` or `post_decision_removal`, so no such fill is ever valued as standing.
- ADVANCED files (3 levels at 1 s) would need code: the `trd` basis is undocumented there, and the queue rule reads full depth.
