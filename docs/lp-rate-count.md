## What it counts

The input is the delayed-key recordings the #41 recorder writes on the lab Mac ([amendment 2](#amendment-2-2026-11-05)): GB/IE, event type 7, WIN, with EX_BEST_OFFERS at 3 levels. The delayed key is 1–180 s late and conflated to 180 s (`betangel_research/betfair/recorder.py:14-15`, facts F44 and F51). The T6 live test recorded Betfair echoing that conflation, 180000 ms (`reports/betfair-live-tests-2026-10-13.json:129`). It is not licensed historical data.

- **Sessions.** It reads every directory under the recordings root named as the recorder names sessions (`rec-YYYYMMDDTHHMMSSZ-` plus 8 hex digits, the UTC start); other names are ignored. A session that started before 2026-11-03 23:05:00 UTC is not read ([amendment 1](#amendment-1-2026-11-04)). A session is read only if its receipt (`receipt.json`, or `receipt.partial.json` while it is still running or after a crash) gives:
  - key_type `delayed` and label `delayed_conflated`;
  - `conflate_ms_echoed` exactly 180000;
  - a subscription that asks for EX_BEST_OFFERS and EX_MARKET_DEF but not EX_BEST_OFFERS_DISP (virtual prices), with ladderLevels 1 to 3.
- **Segments.** In a session that is read, only the segments the receipt lists are read. Each one needs a matching sha256 and a passed replay check. A segment that fails part way through is dropped whole. Every session or segment that is skipped is counted under a fixed code, and so is a `rec-*` entry that is a file or a symlink (symlinks are never followed).
- **Markets.** GB or IE, event type 7, market type WIN, ODDS, one winner, CLASSIC price ladder, at least 5 ACTIVE runners, OPEN and not in play. All of these are checked in the snapshot used. The CLASSIC check follows the order path's own check (`betangel_research/betfair/validate.py:122`).
- **Runner rule (primary).** An ACTIVE runner whose best back b and best lay l are both on the CLASSIC ladder, with:
  - b < l;
  - 1.50 ≤ b ≤ 5.90;
  - l exactly 3 or 4 CLASSIC ticks above b.

  A market qualifies when at least one runner meets the rule.

## Off and snapshots

- **Off.** The off is the first `marketTime` the recordings publish for the market. Sessions are read in start order and segments in index order. Why:
  - It is fixed before any snapshot is chosen. The other option, the marketTime in the last definition at or before the snapshot, depends on the very snapshot it is meant to place.
  - It is the same anchor lp-v1 (`docs/lp-study.md:23`), movement-v1 (`docs/movement-study.md:21`) and the recordings index (`betangel_research/betfair/recordings.py:12-14`) use.
  - The recorder's replay check makes sure every market's first change carries a marketTime (`betangel_research/betfair/recorder.py:125-131`).

  A later revision does not move the off. Markets whose marketTime was revised at or before off −590 s are counted on their own line but not excluded, because the registered rule has no such exclusion.
- **Racing day.** A racing day is a UTC date of the off on which at least one market is usable at −590 s ([amendment 3](#amendment-3-2026-11-09)). A short day, such as one where the lab Mac slept or the recorder crashed early, counts as a full racing day.
- **Snapshot at X.** This is the last recorded state at or before off + X. It holds every message with publish time pt ≤ off + X, so the batch published at off + X is included. Across segments, the latest such state is used.
  - A segment says nothing about a moment after its last line. Heartbeats count here: they arrive every 5 s, so a segment's last line is about when it stopped. If off + X falls after a segment's last line, that segment gives no snapshot at X. A state is never carried into a gap, as the recorder's own rule says. The gap of a few seconds between one segment and the next (a refresh every 30 minutes) can therefore lose a few snapshots, which show as no snapshot.
  - Times are always Betfair's pt, the same clock as marketTime. The lab Mac's `received_at` is never used, because nothing verifies it.
- **Data age.** The age is off + X minus the pt of the last non-heartbeat line (image or delta) at or before off + X, in the segment that holds the state.
  - A snapshot is usable only if its age is at most 180 s, it was taken outside a 503 ("data possibly stale") window, and it carries no non-finite number.
  - Otherwise the market counts as no snapshot (or invalid) at X. An older state is never used instead.
  - Why heartbeats do not count for age: under 180 s conflation, the delta published at T carries every change up to T, so the state is the exchange as of T, minus the 1–180 s delay. A heartbeat only shows that the connection is alive.
  - Why not the market's own last change: a quiet market is not stale.
  - *Inference:* that a delta leaving a market out means the market did not change follows from Betfair's conflation behaviour. It has not been checked on a live stream.
  - In the T6 live test the largest gap between non-heartbeat lines was 180.013 s (`reports/betfair-live-tests-2026-10-13.json:135-138`). That run had only 4 such lines (1 image and 3 deltas). So a few milliseconds in each 180 s may fall just past the limit, but how often is not known from that sample.
- **Non-finite numbers.** A non-finite number on any runner (removed runners included) makes the market invalid until its next image. Images come at the start of each segment, so this can last up to 30 minutes. That is stricter than `book.py`, so it can only remove snapshots. The "invalid" count covers both 503 windows and non-finite numbers, so it is an upper bound on how many this rule removes.

## Columns

The script prints these per racing day and pooled. Every column counts markets usable at −590 s ([amendment 3](#amendment-3-2026-11-09)), and every rate is out of those markets, rounded down to 0.01%.

| Column | Counts the usable markets that are |
| --- | --- |
| usable | in the population, with a usable snapshot at −590 s (the denominator) |
| primary | qualifying at −590 s |
| flagged at −780 s | qualifying in the usable snapshot at −780 s (the last state at or before off −780 s). A proxy for the plain bot's delayed screen, which ends at off −780 s. It is counted only among markets usable at −590 s |
| persistence | primary and flagged at −780 s. Labelled "persistence in delayed data, not live recall" |
| persistence from two batches | persistence, where the −780 s and −590 s snapshots come from different delayed batches |
| window | qualifying in some usable state from −600 s to −300 s: the state at −600 s, or the state after any line published after −600 s and up to −300 s. Description only: the plain bot's quotes rest from about −590 s to the pull at −300 s |
| price lever | with an ACTIVE runner whose 6.0 ≤ b ≤ 10.0 and l is 3 or 4 ticks above |
| width lever | with an ACTIVE runner whose 1.50 ≤ b ≤ 5.90 and l is 5 to 7 ticks above |

- The table also shows "recorded": the markets in the recordings read, with their off on that date.
- In `--json`, every column appears per racing day and pooled, with rates out of the usable markets. The table prints primary, persistence, window and the two levers per day; flagged and two-batch counts appear only in its pooled persistence line, where persistence and the two-batch count are shown as shares of the markets flagged at −780 s. That share is how often a delayed flag still holds at −590 s, at its most favourable reading (*inference*). It is taken only among markets still usable at −590 s: a flagged market that is suspended or under 5 ACTIVE runners by then drops out of both counts. It is taken at the latest screen time: the bot's screen window is off −840 to −780 s. And both snapshots are delayed, while the bot's screen is a delayed book and its decision a live one. So it overstates how often the bot's own flag would survive.
- The −780 s and −590 s snapshots are 190 s apart, more than the 180 s age limit, so a usable −590 s state never comes from the batch seen at −780 s, and the two-batch count equals persistence (*inference* from the age rule; [amendment 3](#amendment-3-2026-11-09)).

It also prints:
- why markets were not usable at −590 s (no snapshot, invalid, out of population, not open or in play, under 5 ACTIVE), over the same dates as the pooled row;
- the revised-marketTime line, over the same dates;
- the sessions and segments it read and skipped, and how many sessions it read from a partial receipt (still running, or crashed);
- the sha256 of the script's own bytes.

It never prints a market id, selection id, event name, venue, price or time of day.

**Until Gate A can be read** it prints only recorded and usable counts, racing days, the not-usable reasons, the revised-marketTime count, the gate's conditions and the inputs. It prints nothing about which markets qualified, so nobody can watch the rate build up through its output.

## Gate A

Gate A is fixed by the recordings, not by when the script is run:

- **Its dates.** Walking the UTC dates of the off in order, Gate A pools every date up to and including the first date D on which the running totals reach 10 racing days and 300 markets usable at −590 s. Dates after D never change it. They are printed per day for description only and are not pooled.
- **When it can be read.** Only once D's recording has ended, and that means one thing: a session directory with a later UTC start date exists under the root ([amendment 4](#amendment-4-proposed-2026-11-10), *proposed*). How D's own sessions stopped is not used. A stop at the end instant or at a cap (`end_at_reached`, `max_duration_reached` or `max_seconds_reached`) ends the recording day only for the daily wrapper, at 23:00 London; a recording started by hand stops the same way at any time of day. A session stopped for live work (`stop_requested`) or for a reconnect restart has not ended the day either: the daily wrapper resumes recording after it. The session names are listed once, before any receipt is read, so a later-date name in that list means D's sessions had stopped before they were read (*inference*: one recorder runs at a time on a profile).
- So every run of the deciding code ([amendment 4](#amendment-4-proposed-2026-11-10), *proposed*) made once such a session exists gives the same PASS or FAIL, and no run before that gives either.

- **PASS.** The primary count is at least 1.4% of the markets usable at −590 s, pooled. The test is exact: primary × 1000 ≥ 14 × usable. Yu then decides whether Pilot 2 is built. The count starts nothing on its own.
- **FAIL.** Below 1.4%. No Pilot 2 code is built unless Yu records a decision to go on anyway. Yu then chooses between:
  - stopping LP here, at a cost of £0; or
  - the lab bringing the levers with their counts (a later decision, up to −300 s, as the window column describes; lays above 6.0 at a smaller stake; wider spreads; or other markets).
- **Built is read as merged.** In PASS and FAIL above, "built" has meant merged since Yu's choice of 2026-11-08: the Pilot 2 code is drafted, and Gate A decides whether it merges ([amendment 4](#amendment-4-proposed-2026-11-10), Built and merged).
- **NOT_ENOUGH_DAYS.** Fewer than 10 racing days. Wait for more recorded days, then run it again.
- **NOT_ENOUGH_MARKETS.** 10 racing days or more, but fewer than 300 usable markets so far. Wait, then run it again.
- **RECORDING_OPEN.** D is known, but no session started on a later UTC date exists yet ([amendment 4](#amendment-4-proposed-2026-11-10), *proposed*). Run it again once the next day's recording has started; the daily wrapper starts at 07:00 London. If no recording is made after D, Gate A stays RECORDING_OPEN until Yu starts one more on a later UTC date. A short one is enough, because only its start date is used. A file or symlink with a session's name is not a session and is ignored here.
- The threshold is 1.4%, about 0.5 qualifying markets a racing day. That is roughly five times the 2015-16 rate of 0.25–0.3% of markets at off −300 s (design, expected_rate). At −600 s the design's same cells hold 27 plus part of 51 a year, about 0.33–0.43% of markets, so at −590 s the threshold is about three to four times the 2015-16 rate (*inference*: part of a cell is estimated, as the design does at −300 s). So if today's markets were like 2015-16's, a FAIL would be the expected result: with 300 usable markets and a true rate of 0.4%, the chance of a PASS is under 1% (*inference*: exact binomial, markets taken as independent; [What it cannot show](#what-it-cannot-show)).
  - That comparison is approximate. lp-v1 excluded markets whose schedule was revised and divided by indexed markets. This count keeps revised markets and divides by markets usable at −590 s. lp-v1 also read the PRO files' full ladders (atb/atl), which are not rolled up, while this count's best prices are rolled up at £1 ([What it cannot show](#what-it-cannot-show), Roll-up). *Inference:* an amount under £1 could set lp-v1's best price, so its spreads are, if anything, narrower than the displayed ones.

## What it cannot show

- **Delay.** The data is delayed by 1–180 s and conflated to 180 s, so a "−590 s" state can describe the exchange as early as about −950 s (*inference*: up to 180 s of age plus up to 180 s of delay).
- **Depth.** It sees the best 3 levels only.
- **Virtual prices.** There are none. Pilot 2 decides on a live book that has them, and virtual prices narrow spreads, which closes some 3–4 tick spreads (`docs/lp-study.md:125`). So a market this count takes can fail the bot's live rule after the bot has opened its live book, with no order sent. The design therefore reads the count as an upper bound, not live recall. It is mostly an upper bound, not a strict one (*inference*): virtual prices could also bring a few wider spreads down into 3–4 ticks, which the width lever shows, and the delay can hide a qualifying spread as well as show one that has gone. The script's Limits line says "mostly an upper bound on the bot's rate" ([amendment 4](#amendment-4-proposed-2026-11-10), *proposed*).
- **Roll-up.** Not a difference between the count and the bot, by Betfair's own pages. The count reads the stream's best-price ladders (batb/batl), and Betfair documents a £1 roll-up for them: "The default roll-up for GBP is £1 for batb / batl and bdatb / bdatl", and "For atb / atl there is no roll-up" (Exchange Stream API, section Currencies, version 196 of 2026-11-03, <https://betfair-developer-docs.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/1smk3cen4v3lu3yomq5qye0ni/pages/2687396>, read 2026-11-10). REST best offers, which the drafted bot reads, default to the same roll-up: `rollupModel` "If unspecified defaults to STAKE rollup model with rollupLimit of minimum stake in the specified currency" (Betting Type Definitions, version 228 of 2025-12-11, <https://betfair-developer-docs.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/1smk3cen4v3lu3yomq5qye0ni/pages/2687465>, read 2026-11-10). So an amount under £1 is shown at the next price in both, and the count does not read the full ladders. Documented by Betfair; not observed by us.
- **The bot's screen.** Gate A's numerator is primary: every market qualifying at −590 s, flagged at −780 s or not. The drafted bot opens a race only if its earlier delayed screen flagged it, so it would pass over the rest. Persistence (primary and flagged) is the nearer count and is never larger. It prints in the same decisive output and is read beside primary. Gate A stays on primary, as registered.
- **Coverage.** The recorder runs 07:00–23:00 London. Explicit-mode subscriptions can leave markets out (the first 150 by start time; `betangel_research/betfair/recorder.py:664-711`). So "recorded" is not every market. Since [amendment 2](#amendment-2-2026-11-05) the recorder also stops while live work is admitted on the lab Mac (`betangel_research/betfair/admission.py:1`). The daily wrapper resumes at most 8 times and restarts at most 2 times a day, and past either cap it records nothing more that day (`betangel_research/betfair/recording_day.py:22-24`). Markets going off in those stretches are in neither the numerator nor the denominator. They are the hours of attended tests, manual drills and, later, Pilot 1 sessions, the same attended hours in which Pilot 2 would run, so those hours are under-sampled. Which way that moves the rate is not known.
- **Fills and money.** It says nothing about whether a quote would fill or pay. It sees one raw, delayed spread per market. It cannot see the bot's own effect (*inference*: once both legs rest inside a 3–4 tick spread, the spread shown is 1 or 2 ticks), who would take each leg, other quotes arriving inside the spread, how long the spread lasts while the legs rest, or how often one leg fills alone.
- **What a PASS shows.** Only that the spread was frequent enough in these recordings to reach 1.4%. It is a frequency check and no more. *Inference* (arithmetic, markets taken as independent; markets on one card are not, so the true spread of results is wider):
  - The smallest PASS is 5 qualifying markets of 300 to 357 usable, and 6 from 358.
  - For 5 of 300 the exact 95% interval is 0.54% to 3.85%.
  - With 330 usable markets the chance of a PASS is about 1% at a true rate of 0.4%, 8% at 0.7%, 24% at 1.0%, 49% at 1.4% and 79% at 2.0%.
  - So a PASS on a handful of markets cannot tell a changed market from chance, or from the count's upward leanings (primary without the bot's screen, and mostly an upper bound).
  - A date with one usable market counts as a racing day, and the rate pools the whole recorded day with no split by time of day.
