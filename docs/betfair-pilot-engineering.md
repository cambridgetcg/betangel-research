## 4. The decision: reservation, evidence and record (Astra #1, #4; R2-2)
One protocol, in this order, inside one pilot placement open (open() to close(), under .gateway.lock):
1. Reserve before fetching. At or after off − 300 s, using the scheduler's off time, the gateway appends a reservation for (contract sha256, market), with a compare-and-append on the journal head (journal.py:17-20, :483-490). It carries the session id and a new open id. A second reservation for the same pair is refused, so two runners cannot both decide one market. One open tries one reservation: once its reservation append has been attempted (written, refused as too late, or its head moved past the retries), that open places nothing more; only a refusal before any append, such as one too early, may be retried in the same open (P106-R2 item 2). The scheduler may find candidates in any catalogue, cached or delayed. That list sets timing only; it is never evidence.
2. Fetch once. After reserving, the gateway itself fetches, with the live key, one fresh, uncached listMarketCatalogue row with the MARKET_DESCRIPTION, EVENT_TYPE, MARKET_START_TIME and EVENT projections, then one decision book with isMarketDataDelayed false. In that row, off is marketStartTime (the same time the send book reads as description.marketTime, api.py:956) and country is event.countryCode; the event's openDate is never the off. The normal snapshot has no country (api.py:46, :950-962), catalogue_row() may serve a row up to 6 h old (api.py:633-653; config.py:46), and the read slot defaults to delayed (config.py:44); none of them is used. A caller-supplied book, catalogue or country is refused. Raw responses go to the state root and their digests are journaled, whatever the outcome. There is no second fetch. Each raw read is one transport attempt with no retry or relogin, bounded by orders.pilot.evidence_deadline_s (5 s, in the registered config), with both clocks read before and after it; a read that fails, returns a non-200 status, takes longer than that on either clock, runs backwards or lets the clocks drift apart by more than 2 s is SKIP(evidence_invalid) on the exact bytes received (none: the empty body), its cause in the evidence sidecar (P1-06 Q8, Q10). A classify refusal journals both digests too.
3. Classify once. The gateway checks the P1 predicates below on that evidence and appends one decision record, PLACE or SKIP with a code, by compare-and-append. A PLACE takes its capacity in the same append (§6). If the head moved, the gateway re-checks capacity, stops and the pause (§10) on the new head with the same evidence and appends again. It never fetches again.
4. Interrupted means SKIP. Only the open that made a reservation may classify it. A reservation whose open ended without a record (crash, kill, lost lock) is SKIP(interrupted) in every fold. It is never refetched or re-decided. The journal shows that an open has ended when another open reserves, or with an open_ended note naming it, written under .gateway.lock, from a journal read inside that hold, by a process that knows nobody can still act for that open: pilot-run between its own opens, or another process holding the live owner lock (pilot-run holds that lock for its whole run, so it is not running).
- The P1 predicates. This list governs; contract p1 holds the same values. "off" is marketStartTime in the fresh catalogue row (step 2).
  - market: country GB or IE; event type 7; market type WIN; ODDS; one winner; CLASSIC ladder; at least 5 ACTIVE runners in the decision book;
  - window: the decision book was observed from off − 300 s to off − 300 s + 15 s. Outside it: SKIP(window);
  - selection: the only ACTIVE runner with the lowest best back price (a tie is SKIP); best back from 1.50 to 4.00; at least £10 available at it; best lay no more than 2 ticks above it;
  - order: BACK, LIMIT at exactly that best back price, LAPSE, stake 200p.
- The record holds: contract sha256; registration receipt sha256; session id; open id; reservation seq; market and selection ids; side; limit price in hundredths; stake; LAPSE; country and off from the fresh row; the decision book's time on both clocks and its isMarketDataDelayed flag; the send deadline (off − 240 s); digests of the row and the book; PLACE or SKIP with a code; for a PLACE, its lifetime slot and day count.
- Two books, two jobs. The decision book alone decides the selection, depth and spread. The send book is the gateway's later fresh book (A18). On it the gateway runs V0-V14 and V9b as today and V13 (no more than 2 ticks of movement). It checks that the selection is still ACTIVE, and refuses if the send book's market_time differs from the record's off. It never re-decides from the send book.
- Binding. R13 checks the body against the PLACE record from this same open, field by field. The record's sha256 goes into the risk decision, and the capability binds it together with the body sha256 (gateway.py:1234-1246). Missing, contradictory or reused evidence refuses: a record from another open, session or contract; a digest that does not match; a body that differs.

## 5. The first-byte gate (Astra #2; R2-1)
- After a PLACE, the place path runs as today: funds, send book, V-rules, R1-R13, intent, risk decision, then the submit, compare-and-appended on the head the risk decision saw (gateway.py:1104-1116, :1241-1246). PA4 is checked at open, at classification and at the risk decision.
- Right before the first request byte, the gate (gateway.py:915-934) refuses unless all six hold:
  1. no KILL file (as today);
  2. the send book is within max_book_age_s on both clocks (as today), and its wall-clock age measured at the time check 4 uses (the latest this open has seen) is within it too, an upper bound only: a wall clock that stepped back cannot make the book look fresher than the open has already seen it (P106-R3-1). The negative-age rule here is the raw one above: the gate's own reading before the book's observed_at refuses;
  3. a fresh read of the journal head, on its own read-only connection (journal.head_fresh; the gateway's own connection can return a stale head while a read is open), is exactly the head our own submit created. Any later event from any writer refuses: HALT, kill, kill_incomplete, a pilot stop or anything else. An unreadable head, or one that does not follow from our submit, also refuses;
  4. the wall clock is before the send deadline and before session expiry (the earlier of UNTIL and 23:59:59Z), the final date and the next record time trigger; the clock used is the largest of the wall clock, the submit's recorded_at and every time this placement open observed, each of its own events' recorded_at, every clock read of the open and both ends of both raw reads included (P106-1, P106-C2). Every wall-clock read of the open, by the gateway or by any part it uses (the journal, the risk guard, the JSON-RPC and read clients, the session), goes through one shared pilot_gate.ObservedClock, which reports it to that open's high-water mark; a pilot gateway whose parts do not share one is refused when it is built, and P1-08's CLI builds one and hands it to each part (P106-R2-1). So a wall clock that stepped back cannot hide an append stamped late, and a session end this open has observed never becomes live again within it: every authority check of the open (the reservation, the classification, the risk decision, the submit) uses that time. At the risk decision, the risk fold is read first, then one raw reading for the decision. Staleness is judged at the retained time, an upper bound: V9b, V10's wall-clock book age and R11's funds age (P106-R3-1). The raw reading judges two things: the R6/R7 windows, so a transient forward reading cannot move a window's start forward (the reservation's R6 check likewise uses its own raw reading, round 4); and the negative ages, so a book or funds read after the raw reading refuses (V10 book_age_negative, R11), as does one read after the retained time. Evidence keeps its raw stamps, and a manual order keeps the raw clock alone. A wall step back of more than max_book_age_s after an earlier reading of the open therefore refuses at the risk decision (V10, a review stop) before the drift check would see it, and a one-off backward raw reading at the risk decision refuses as a negative age (V10, a review stop). What the raw readings leave (P1-08 §14): R6 is judged at two raw readings, the reservation's and the risk decision's, so a single transient moves at most one of them; R7 is judged at the risk decision's raw reading only, acceptable at a cap of 300 an hour against 25 a day; both trust earlier submits' recorded_at. Before the reservation's R6/R7 check, a mark more than max_book_age_s ahead of the raw reading refuses as V10 book_stale_wall_clock, a review stop, so a step back that stays is surfaced there (R5-1). Otherwise a step back is caught at the risk decision only when the book's age at the mark exceeds max_book_age_s and no earlier raw-judged check refuses first; else check 2 or check 6 catches it at the first byte, for that market only. Known and left as is: R6 counts submits by their own recorded_at, so a single backward reading at an earlier submit's stamp can age that submit out of a later window (a pacing cap, not a money cap; shared with the manual path);
  5. the monotonic time since the decision book fits inside the same remaining window;
  6. the wall and monotonic clocks have not drifted apart by more than 2 s since the decision (as order-path:424 does).
- Order inside the gate (P106-C1). The head read for check 3 is I/O, so the gate does it first; checks 1, 2 and 4-6 then read the KILL file and both clocks after it (an unreadable head is acted on only after check 1, so a KILL file still ends the session, R2-N2), and the transport checks its absolute send-by (the book's freshness, capped by the capability's expiry) again after the gate returns, right before the first byte. A delayed head read therefore sends nothing.
- Why check 3. Holding .gateway.lock does not freeze the journal. kill takes no lock (gateway.py:2156). If it cannot create the KILL file, it still journals the kill and the HALT (gateway.py:2170-2180, :2138-2148). The CLI can also append HALT kill_incomplete without the lock (cli.py:1408-1416). So a stop can be committed while no KILL file exists. Check 3 sees it. The lock, the body binding, the clocks and the KILL check all stay; none of them replaces check 3.
- What is left. A stop committed after check 3's read and before the first byte cannot be seen. If that stop is a kill invocation, the kill attempts re-sweeps at T0 + 20 s and T0 + 35 s (gateway.py:2157-2158; killswitch.py:228-237). They are attempts, not a guarantee: a network or login failure can stop them. A file-only KILL, a HALT or a pilot stop sweeps nothing. In every such case, including a send whose outcome is UNKNOWN, the executor's remainder cancel or its handoff to Yu (§8) follows. If neither the KILL file nor the HALT can be written, nothing on the lab Mac can stop a send: use the Betfair website or phone app.
- A long open() cannot stretch the window. The 120 s reconcile budget (gateway.py:102), the lock wait, the Keychain anchor and the login all count against it. The executor opens early enough to reserve at off − 300 s.
- Authority at the append (P106-2). Every pilot reservation, decision and submit is a compare-and-append on the head it was judged on, and is also refused, with nothing written, unless its own recorded_at (stamped before the write lock is taken), and the wall clock read again once the lock is held and again just before COMMIT, are before the authority boundary (the commit's flush may still finish later; the fold judges recorded_at, and the first-byte gate re-checks a submit): the session's end, the final date and the next record time trigger (and, for the submit, the send deadline). A late reservation or decision is never written; a reservation already written stays SKIP(interrupted); a late submit is refused (pilot_submit_too_late) and its intent REFUSED. A 30 s margin and a trigger horizon at reservation are optimisations in front of this check, not the check.
- Any refusal here is FAILED(transport_not_sent): no byte is written, the market stays reserved and the slot stays used (§6). So the end of a session, a KILL, a HALT or a due stop voids every placement not yet sent, not just the next loop. The one exception is the race above: a placement whose checks passed just before the stop was committed can still be sent.

## 6. Capacity, durable stops and carryover (Astra #4)
- The journal is the authority. Pilot records are note events. A note enforces nothing by itself, so the pilot fold and compare-and-append rules enforce everything in §4 and here.
- R13, not R8, enforces one decision per market. R8 frees up once exposure is released (risk.py:374-375), and the journal is unique per order ref, not per market (journal.py:125).
- Every reservation uses up its market, SKIP included. No reservation is ever released: not after REFUSED, FAILED, UNKNOWN, cancel, settle, restart or RESUME.
- A PLACE record takes one of the 495 lifetime slots and one day slot in its own append, before anything can be sent. If either is full, the record is SKIP(cap). Day limits: 5 PLACE records on each PT4 session day (§14), then 25 per UTC day. They count PLACE records, not R6's submitted instructions (risk.py:301-324). A slot whose request was definitely not sent stays used.
- At the day limit the scheduler ends the day rather than reserving a market only to SKIP(cap). The one exception is a reservation whose PLACE would fire a pause: it still goes ahead, so SKIP(pause) and its stops are journaled as §10 says (P1-08 Q16). No limit changes: the record's own append still enforces both caps.
- Pilot stops are folded from the journal, so a restart cannot lose them. Session stop: ends today's session; the next needs a new ARM line. Review stop: no placement until §12's conditions for resuming hold and Yu types `PILOT ACK <contract12> <stop-id>`; for a pause, §10 says what is needed and what the ACK covers. Contract ended: final. `resume` and RESUME LIVE clear a gateway HALT only. They cannot clear a review stop or reopen an ended contract.
- Carryover. Reservations, slots, day counts, stops, acknowledged pause levels and the ledger's equity history (§10) are keyed by contract sha256. A new registration of the same contract (for example after a code fix) keeps all of them. A replacement contract (for example after PT4 forces a contract change) names its predecessor's sha256 and inherits all of them: the slots left (495 minus those used), every reservation, any active stop, the acknowledged pause levels and the equity history from the first registration. So the £990 worst case holds across both.
- A fresh journal on the lab Mac (Yu, 2026-11-05; §2). A13 lets a journal take its first Keychain anchor only while it holds no order and no ref_roundtrip_verified (arming.py:136-153), so the lab Mac starts with an empty journal. The Air's state root, journal and anchor are never copied to the lab Mac: order-path §7's restore rule makes any copy a recovery, with no ARM, place or live-probe until Yu and Astra have established what was lost. Carryover therefore holds only within one journal, and the move starts only once the Air's journal holds no PLACE record or pilot note (§14). Yu confirmed the fresh journal on 2026-11-06 (relayed). The Air lineage ends at seq 28, last_sha256 ecfa00cb… (the full values are in the plan's Architecture), and a read-only copy of the Air's state root stays on the Air.

## 7. Registration receipt and identity (Astra #5)
- The contract JSON in Git holds the rule, caps, N, metric, exclusions, analysis, stops, final date and strategy ref. It names no commit, so committing it cannot change what it names.
- `pilot-register` writes a registration receipt outside the contract, in the state root, and journals its sha256. The receipt binds: the contract sha256; the code commit and tree; the receipts.code_hashes digest (receipts.py:121-125); and the full config sha256 (the daily token uses its first 12 hex, arming.py:82).
- The receipt (P1-06 Q4). `STATE_ROOT/profiles/<p>/pilot/registration-<contract12>-<UTC compact>.json`, mode 0600, canonical indented JSON with exactly these keys: `schema` ("betangel.pilot-registration/v1"), `contract_sha256`, `code_commit` and `code_tree` (40 or 64 lowercase hex), `code_hashes_digest` (the canonical sha256 of receipts.code_hashes), `config_sha256`, `registered_at` (UTC text) and `journal_seq` (the journal head's seq when it was written; the registration note follows it). The registration note and every session_start carry the sha256 of the file's bytes, and every pilot placement open finds the receipt by that digest (pilot_gate.read_registration). P1-08's pilot-register writes it with pilot_gate.REGISTRATION_SCHEMA and registration_path; P1-06 only reads and verifies it.
- Every pilot placement open checks all four for exact equality, and that the tree is clean. An unknown or unreadable identity, or a dirty tree, refuses. Caps inside bounds is not enough. Cleanup commands keep today's checks (§8), so drift never blocks cleanup.
- The code identity is pinned once per process, when the gateway's pilot adapter is first imported, from the module root the running package came from; every placement open requires that pin, the files on disk now and the receipt to agree, and the checked repository to be that module root. Python gives no digest of the code it actually loaded, so a file changed between its import and the pin, or a stale bytecode cache, is not seen.
- customer_strategy_ref stays p1-fav-t5 until every pilot order is settled, even after the contract ends. A kill on an untrusted journal then still sweeps pilot bets (gateway.py:390, :2189-2194).
- Manual orders under the pilot profile (P1-06 critique 13; Yu 2026-10-21; Astra's ruling). With orders.pilot in the config, every order that is not the pilot's carries the fixed ref bar-manual, bound by its source into the risk decision and the body check; only source "pilot" carries the config's p1-fav-t5. The emergency sweep of such a profile names both refs from the config alone, even when the journal, the receipt or the contract cannot be read; cleanup has no PA gate. While any pilot session in the journal has not reached its end (UNTIL or 23:59:59Z, a session a stop ended early included), live manual place and live-probe are refused, read from the journal alone, with or without orders.pilot or the pilot context. The same check runs again on the journal read a live manual place's decision basis comes from, so a session armed after the first check refuses the order before its intent. A manual or other non-pilot bet of ours that is still open or matched and unsettled when the pilot path finds an armed session (at a pilot-place open or before a reservation) makes it journal a HALT, manual_bet_open_during_session, which ends the session (the page: "if the code finds one, it halts"; R12 sees only exposure the journal cannot explain, so it does not catch a bet the gateway journaled); placing needs RESUME and a new ARM once the bet is cancelled or settled. These are the only changes to non-pilot behaviour; with no pilot section and no pilot session in the journal, nothing changes.
- code_hashes covers only betangel_research/*.py and betangel_research/betfair/*.py. It catches drift. It does not prove Yu is present, and it does not stop a hostile process (order-path:7).
- A code or config change needs a new receipt, Astra's acceptance and the go-live consent again (§14), naming the new code commit and config sha256. Config holds more than caps (for example orders.customer_strategy_ref, config.py:57; order_deadline_s and the login caps, config.py:41-43). §6 carries the state over.

- The remainder cancel during a KILL (R3-1). A KILL file sweeps nothing. A kill invocation journals kill and halt when it can, but it journals an outcome only when it ends incomplete (HALT kill_incomplete: gateway.py:2214-2216; cli.py:1405-1411). A complete kill journals none. So, for a sent pilot order whose place report shows an executable remainder, the executor reads only the journal, not Betfair, and takes the first case that applies:
  1. It cannot read the journal: it prints the handoff at once.
  2. No kill event after its submit (a file-only KILL, or a kill that could not journal): it sends its own whole cancel at once.
  3. A kill event after its submit, then HALT kill_incomplete: it sends its own whole cancel at once.
  4. A kill event after its submit, with no outcome: a sweep may be running. It sends no cancel and makes no Betfair read, because either would compete with the sweep (gateway.py:1300-1301, :1310-1312; F18). It waits up to 30 s from when it first sees that kill event. If HALT kill_incomplete appears, it acts as in case 3. If not, it prints the handoff at once. A complete kill and a kill process that died look the same here, which is why the wait is bounded. The kill's last re-sweep is at T0 + 35 s, so the handoff can come while a healthy kill is still running. Following it is still safe.
- An UNKNOWN place outcome. A cancel needs a bet id (gateway.py:1371, :1382-1383), and the place path stops reconciling once a KILL file exists (gateway.py:1309-1322; order-path:194). ## 10. The ledger, equity and the pause (Astra #3; R2-3)
- The ledger is a pure fold of journaled pilot records, kept on the lab Mac. It is separate from the R-rules, which keep their own worst case, and it is never fed back into them. It can only bring a stop earlier.
- One equation: equity = the sum of one value per PLACE record. Each record is in exactly one part:
  - W, worst case, the default: minus the final matched stake if the final execution record (§11) gives it. Before that record is final, minus the matched size of the canonical C read showing EXECUTION_COMPLETE (§11), but only once that read has passed the capture check in §11 (run when the read is captured, not at finalization); nothing more can match after EXECUTION_COMPLETE. A sent order with no place report, or whose C read failed the capture check or any §11 consistency check, stays at minus £2.00 until its final record. Otherwise minus £2.00. So a bet that never matched counts £0 from its checked EXECUTION_COMPLETE read, and a resolved zero match is £0. A record with no submit event, or FAILED(transport_not_sent), is a resolved zero match: nothing was sent (order-path:183). Until the journal shows that a record's open has ended (§4), the pause, checked after each ledger change as the fold reads the journal in order, counts a record with no submit, no FAILED(transport_not_sent) and no evidence of sending at −£2.00; from that point it counts £0, as a fold taken now already does once the open has closed. So a pause that would fire only because of that −£2.00, after an open_ended note and before the next reservation, does not fire: a stop that fires less often, which Yu accepted in plain words (2026-10-22, P1-10 Q1).
  - Contradiction restores the worst case. If any later execution evidence for the bet (a later C read, an L row, a place, cancel or reconcile report, or startup reconciliation) shows a larger matched size than the basis the ledger used for that record, or otherwise contradicts that basis, whether the record was in W or in S, the record moves to W at minus £2.00 at once and a review stop is set. This also applies after the measurement record is final; it does not rewrite the final analytical record (§11), only the safety ledger. The fold re-derives it from the journal, so a restart gives the same answer.
  - This ledger-only use of a current-order row differs from the order path's rule that exchange rows never lower a counted worst case (order-path §8). That rule still governs the R-rules and exposure; the ledger is separate and never feeds them (above). The capture check is what makes the ledger's use safe: a row that shows less matched than our own place report, or sizes that do not add up to £2.00, is never used.
  - S, settled and verified, only when all hold: Yu's typed settle-market for the market succeeded; the final execution record is resolved; the CLOSED read shows one WINNER, our runner WINNER or LOSER, no void and no runner removed after the decision; Betfair's per-bet profit is present and the commission check below passed; and a PT4 receipt with at least one matched, settled bet (§14) exists.
- S values. LOSER: minus the final matched stake. WINNER: gross = final matched size × (P − 1), P at full precision, floored to the penny; commission = c × the positive market gross, rounded up to the penny, where c is the one rate frozen in contract p1, 2% (Betfair's rate on Yu's account), which the plan's metric also uses (one bet per market; pnl.py:32-34, :79-83; no discount assumed); net = gross − commission. The value is the lower of our net and Betfair's per-bet profit minus that commission.
- Like-for-like checks. Our per-bet gross is compared with Betfair's per-bet profit, as compare_profit does (pnl.py:87-93), on the sign and basis PT1 or PT4 verified (F37 is unverified, facts.md:187). Our commission is compared with Betfair's market commission where the API returns it; F37 doubts that it does. Where it does not, the commission check passes only after Yu has matched our commission with the website's, to the penny, for a winning pilot market (in PT4 or by a later written check). A net figure is never compared with a gross one.
- A gap over 1p in either check keeps the bet in W and sets a review stop, from the first bet, PT4 included. A missing Betfair figure keeps the bet in W until it arrives; if it never arrives, the bet stays in W.
- These stay in W for the whole contract: a dead heat (pnl.order_pnl_pence pays the full win, pnl.py:24-35; market_realized refuses it, pnl.py:53-56); a void; any runner removed after the decision; an unknown result, price or size; any settlement we cannot verify. Five ordinary PT4 bets do not validate them. Learning their basis needs a later approved contract; it cannot change Pilot 1.
- Pinned examples (each is a test). Pending: £2.00 fully matched, not settled: −£2.00. Never matched: a PLACE whose canonical C read shows EXECUTION_COMPLETE with nothing matched: £0, though its measurement class stays pending to the deadline (§11). Ordinary winner: £2.00 matched at 3.00, settled, Betfair profit 400p, commission 8p verified: +£3.92. Unresolved settlement: the same bet with Betfair's profit missing, or 3p apart: −£2.00, and the 3p gap sets a review stop. Contradiction (a test, with a restart): the place report shows £0 matched; canonical C1 is EXECUTION_COMPLETE with £0 matched and £2 cancelled, so W is £0; a later C2 shows EXECUTION_COMPLETE with £1 matched and £1 cancelled. C2 contradicts C1, so W returns to −£2.00 and a review stop is set; with other equity at −£149 the total is −£151, which also fires the −£150 pause. The final analytical record still follows §11. Never sent: a PLACE refused by V13 before its submit, or FAILED(transport_not_sent): £0. 485 settled £2 losers plus ten pending £2 bets: equity −£990, never −£1,010.
- The pause (Yu's number on the page: −£150, and each further −£150). The pause levels are −£150, −£300, −£450 and so on, on the one equity equation, counted from the first registration (§6 carries equity and the acknowledged levels over). They are measured from £0, never from a peak, and they never move. One pure function checks them at every PLACE classification, counting the new bet at −£2.00, and after every ledger change:
  - a PLACE whose −£2.00 would take equity to or below a level not yet acknowledged becomes SKIP(pause);
  - a ledger change that leaves equity at or below a level not yet acknowledged (for example a failed consistency check moving a bet back to −£2.00) has the same effect at once;
  - either way a session stop and a review stop are journaled at once, and placing waits until Yu has looked and types `PILOT ACK <contract12> <stop-id>`, which is the page's "go on". The stop-id names the level. A pause is not a fault, so it needs no written cause (§12); every other §12 condition (journal reconciled, no unresolved order, no open ledger gap) still holds before the ACK is accepted.
- What an ACK covers (R3-4, revised for the pause). An ACK acknowledges that one level for the contract's whole life. Placing may then go on until the next level, £150 further down. An acknowledged level never fires again, even if equity recovers above it and falls back. A level below it is never waived by it. Equity is never reset and caps are never edited. The fold rebuilds levels, stops and ACKs from the journal, so a restart gives the same answer.
- Pinned example (a test, with a restart): settled equity −£148, nothing pending. The next PLACE would make equity −£150: SKIP(pause), a session stop and a review stop. Yu looks and ACKs. In the next session the next PLACE, which takes equity from −£148 to −£150 again, is not a pause SKIP; after a restart, the same. The −£300 level stays armed: a PLACE that would take equity to −£300 is SKIP(pause). If equity later recovers to +£4 and falls back to −£150, the acknowledged −£150 level does not fire.
- There is no separate drawdown stop, HALT threshold or kill threshold. The 495 lifetime cap is what bounds the total: 495 × £2 = £990.
- Outside the code: a Betfair website loss limit of £50 a day, and a balance kept close to the budget, with no top-ups. The £499 key and running costs are not trading losses.

## 11. Final execution, BSP and measurement classes (Astra #7; R2-4, R2-5)
- The place report's average is floored to hundredths for risk (gateway.py:195; reconcile.py:128-134). That stays for risk; the metric never uses it. More can match before the cancel lands (lifecycle.py:283-288).
- Each PLACE gets one final execution record from two sources. C, the current row: listCurrentOrders once the bet is EXECUTION_COMPLETE (after the remainder cancel, a full match or the lapse at the off), read before its market settles, because settled bets leave that list (F77); sizeMatched and averagePriceMatched kept at full precision. L, the cleared row: listClearedOrders after Betfair settles; sizeSettled, priceMatched and priceReduced. An L row counts as read only once a PT1 or PT4 receipt has verified those fields against the website (F37). An L row read before that is logged, and counts from that receipt if the receipt comes before the deadline. If a receipt finds that the fields mean something else, no later session is armed until a replacement contract (§6) fixes the method.
- The capture check (for the ledger, §10). When a C read showing EXECUTION_COMPLETE is captured, it is checked at once: its sizeMatched is at least the place report's, and its matched + cancelled + lapsed + voided sizes equal £2.00 exactly. A read that fails sets a review stop, is never used by the ledger, and makes the size unresolved as below.
- The place and cancel reports are earlier snapshots. They are checked only for consistency: the final matched size is at least the place report's, and matched + cancelled + lapsed + voided size equals £2.00. Those four sizes come from C when C is read, not from the cancel report: a kill's sweep cancels on a no-orders stand-in, so our own cancel report may not exist (gateway.py:2195-2199). A failed check makes the size unresolved and sets a review stop (§9). They are never compared with final totals as if taken at the same instant.
- Agreement is exact (R3-3). C's and L's sizes and prices are the numbers as written in the raw JSON, parsed with Decimal (never through float). They agree only if they are exactly equal, with no rounding. Contract p1 freezes this rule. A rounding rule may replace it only if Betfair's documentation justifies one, never because of the digits seen in a few fills, and only in a later contract. PT1 and PT4 record C and L as evidence under this rule; they cannot choose a tolerance. A difference that looks like rounding (C 3.444, L 3.44) is a mismatch.
- Finalization (R3-2). A bet already classed void or post_decision_removal is not price-compared: a documented removal comes first, even when C and L differ (class 4). Otherwise this table decides. The deadline is off + 7 days. "L unavailable" means only that no L row counted as read by the deadline; nothing declares it earlier.

| Read by the deadline | Final when | Final size and P (the NCLV price) |
|---|---|---|
| C and L: sizes and prices exactly equal, priceReduced false | when L is read | L's sizeSettled and priceMatched |
| C and L: anything else | when L is read | none: measurement_unresolved |
| C only | at the deadline; pending until then | C's sizeMatched and averagePriceMatched |
| L only | when L is read | L's sizeSettled and priceMatched if priceReduced is false; else measurement_unresolved |
| neither | at the deadline | none: measurement_unresolved |

- Canonical observation (R4-2). When a source has more than one qualifying observation, the canonical one is the earliest by durable capture sequence in the journal (never by loading order). For C, that is the earliest read showing EXECUTION_COMPLETE. For L, it is the earliest logged L row; it becomes usable from the PT receipt, and its place in the sequence does not change. Later observations of the same source are logged as corrections and never replace the canonical one. Pinned: two C reads (3.44, then 3.440000000000001) before L 3.44 gives agreement on the first C; two L rows logged before the receipt, loaded in either order, give the same result.
- Pending is waiting, not failure. An L read before the deadline turns a C-only record into a C-and-L one. Once a record is final, later reads and corrections (C, L, CLOSED or BSP) are logged and never change it. Unresolved never means unmatched. The record never lowers exposure. A bet with no matched part has no L row: L is the SETTLED view, and lapsed or cancelled bets sit in other views (F35, F36, facts.md:164-165; api.py:713-714). So its measurement stays C-only and pending until the deadline, and N is found later. The ledger (§10) already counts it at £0 from its EXECUTION_COMPLETE read; that changes no class.
- Pinned examples (each is a test). C now, then an L tomorrow that contradicts it: final when L is read, measurement_unresolved. C now, no L by the deadline: final at the deadline from C. PT fills all at whole-number prices, then C 3.44 and L 3.06: unresolved. C 3.444 and L 3.44: unresolved. C and L both 3.44: P is L's 3.44.
- BSP comes from raw listMarketBook with SP_TRADED (api.py:571-606); the normalised snapshots drop sp (api.py:935-939). The frozen value is actualSP from the first raw read that shows the market CLOSED, if that read is within off + 24 h and valid: finite and above 1, our runner WINNER or LOSER in it, and bspReconciled true. Otherwise the bet has no BSP. NaN, Infinity and a missing value count as none. Reads before CLOSED, and corrections after it, are logged and never used.
- Capture and custody. In a session the executor reads C once each pilot order is EXECUTION_COMPLETE (after its remainder cancel, a full match or the lapse at the off, as above), then the CLOSED book and L once the market's off has passed; every capture read runs between placement opens, never inside one. The CLOSED book is read for every PLACE record's market, sent or not, so that the decision-price series has each record's BSP; for a record with no bet id it is read only until no read can change it: until off + 24 h for an unsent record, and until the seven-day deadline for a sent one. After the session Yu runs pilot-capture the same evening, then once a day until every record is final or past its deadline. pilot-capture is read only and gives no placement authority. Raw responses stay in the state root until the terminal analysis has been recomputed.
- Measurement classes. Each PLACE record ends in exactly one class; the first that applies wins:
  1. protocol_violation: it broke RS-19 or the contract;
  2. definitely_unsent: no submit event, or FAILED(transport_not_sent);
  3. void: the market was voided, or our runner was removed;
  4. post_decision_removal: another runner ACTIVE in the decision book was later removed (seen in the CLOSED read or in L). It applies whether or not a reduction factor changed our price, and it is decided before any price comparison;
  5. measurement_unresolved: the final matched size, or a matched bet's price, is unresolved;
  6. unmatched: the final matched size is 0;
  7. missing_bsp: no frozen BSP;
  8. eligible.
- A class is final once its execution record is final under the table and the BSP rule has decided, and at off + 7 days at the latest. Anything still unknown then is measurement_unresolved. Later reads never change a final class.

## 15. How the exception ends
- It covers only the contract sha256 named in Yu's go-live consent, run from the code and config in its registration receipt.
- Placing ends at the first of: N reached (contract p1's primary sample is complete: its first 400 eligible bets in decision order are final, and so is every earlier PLACE record); the lifetime cap (495 PLACE records); the final date 2027-06-30; any breach of RS-19 (a bet it does not allow, a session Yu did not start, or a money number changed without Yu); Yu ending the pilot in writing. Stopping one session does not end it. A missed session summary or a missing explanation is reported and put right, not an ending; that never covers a record needed to authorize, bind, count or stop an order (a reservation, decision record, capacity append, submit, or stop): a send without one is a protocol violation. An interrupted reservation (SKIP(interrupted), §4) or an expected missing observation (for example missing BSP, §11) is not. The analysis's validity gates never end placing early. A protocol violation ends it at once.
- After that there is no new reservation or placement. The executor may finish the remainder cancel of an order already sent. Cancel, kill, reconcile, pilot-capture and typed settle-market stay available until the data lock (§12) has passed and no pilot order remains. Then AGENTS.md:9-10 apply exactly as at 416702f.
- The verdict rules are frozen in contract p1. In short: an ending for a breach of RS-19 is a protocol violation and gives INVALID. Any other ending gives INCONCLUSIVE: insufficient_eligible_sample unless INVALID applies, except that records placed before the ending may still complete N by the data lock (late completion), and then the test runs. There is never an early PASS, and the lifetime cap is never raised to finish.
